What to engrave on a cutting board: wording ideas for weddings, housewarmings and anniversaries

Art by NuellaCalgary · November 6, 2026 · 3 min read

An engraved cutting board is one of those gifts that gets used for years. It sits on the counter, comes out for every family dinner and quietly reminds people who gave it. The hardest part is usually not the board. It is deciding what to put on it. Here are some ideas to get you started.

Start with the occasion

The occasion tells you what kind of words fit. A wedding board often celebrates two people becoming a family. A housewarming board welcomes someone to a new place. An anniversary board marks how far a couple has come. Once you know the feeling you want, the wording gets much easier.

Wording ideas by occasion

Here are a few lines that work well on wood. Mix and match, or use them as a starting point:

  • Wedding: the couple's last name with "Est." and the wedding year, or both first names with the wedding date
  • Housewarming: "Welcome home" with the family name, or the street name and the year they moved in
  • Anniversary: both names with "Together since" and the year, or the number of years in a simple line
  • For a cook in the family: "From Grandma's kitchen," "Made with love" or a favourite family saying
  • Holidays: the family name with a short seasonal greeting that still works all year

Keep the layout simple

Less is more on a cutting board. A name, a date and one short line is usually plenty. Long sentences get small and hard to read, and they crowd the wood grain. If you want to include a longer message, a card tucked in with the gift is a nice way to do it.

Think about where the engraving will sit, too. Many people use one side for chopping and keep the engraved side for display or serving. A design placed in a corner or along the bottom edge leaves room to use the board without cutting through the words.

Quick check Read your wording out loud. If it takes more than a breath to say, try trimming it.

Get the details right

Small mistakes are the ones people notice later. Before you send your wording, check the spelling of every name, including any accents. Confirm the exact date and how you want it written. Decide on first names, last names or both. Look closely at capital letters and punctuation, especially for family names with apostrophes.

It also helps to tell us if a name is commonly misspelled. We would rather ask a question than guess.

Pairing the board with other pieces

A cutting board makes a lovely gift on its own, but it also pairs well with a few extras. Some people add matching coasters, a set of engraved utensils or a small basket of kitchen treats. If you are giving it at a wedding shower or housewarming, a few simple pieces together can feel like a complete set.
