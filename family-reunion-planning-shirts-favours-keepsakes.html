Family reunion planning: shirts, favours and keepsakes

Art by NuellaCalgary · November 16, 2026 · 3 min read

A family reunion brings together people who may not see each other for years. Cousins meet for the first time. Grandparents see the whole family in one place. The personal touches you plan help turn a busy weekend into something people talk about long after. Here is a simple way to plan the shirts, favours and keepsakes without getting overwhelmed.

Build one design for the whole event

Start with a single design and use it everywhere. It might be the family name, a tree with roots, the place the family comes from or the year of the reunion. Once you have it, the same design can go on shirts, favours, signs and keepsakes. It ties the whole event together and makes every photo look planned.

Ask a few relatives for input early. A design that came from the family feels more meaningful than one picked in a hurry by one person.

Shirts that bring everyone together

Matching shirts are often the first thing people think of, and for good reason. They make it easy to spot family at a park or hall, and they look great in the group photo. Some families pick one colour for everyone. Others give each branch of the family its own colour with the same design, which makes it easy to see who belongs to which side.

Collect names and sizes with a simple shared form or a group chat. Assign one person to chase the stragglers.

Favours guests will take home

Favours are a small thank you for making the trip. The best ones are useful and carry the reunion design so they become a reminder of the day. Ideas that work well:

  • Tumblers or water bottles with the family name and year
  • Keychains with the family crest or tree
  • Coasters engraved with where the family is from
  • Tote bags for carrying snacks and photos home
  • Notebooks for kids to collect autographs from relatives

Pick one or two. Too many favours can feel scattered.

A centre keepsake for the family

Think about one piece that represents the whole family and lives on after the event. A large engraved plaque with the family tree, a photo crystal of the oldest generation or a signed board that everyone writes on during the day can all work. Some families pass the keepsake to whoever hosts the next reunion.

Idea Set the keepsake on a table near the entrance so everyone sees it, signs it or takes a photo with it.

Honour the elders

The elders are often the reason everyone shows up. A small personal gift for grandparents or the oldest relatives is a lovely way to say thank you. An engraved piece with their names, a framed photo of all their descendants or a crystal of a favourite old picture can mean a lot.
