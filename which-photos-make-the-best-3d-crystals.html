Which photos make the best 3D crystals: a simple checklist

Art by NuellaCalgary · November 6, 2026 · 3 min read

A 3D crystal turns a favourite picture into something you can hold and turn in the light. Because the image becomes a small sculpture inside the glass, some photos work better than others. You do not need a professional photo. You just need the right one. Use this checklist before you send yours.

Look at the lighting first

Light shapes a face. A photo taken in soft, even light, like near a window or outside on a cloudy day, usually shows features clearly. Harsh sun can create deep shadows under the eyes and nose, and flash can flatten everything. If the face looks natural and easy to see on your screen, that is a good sign.

Check the angle and the crop

A photo where people face the camera, or turn just slightly, tends to work best. Strong side profiles or faces looking down can lose detail. Leave a little space around the heads in the frame. A photo cropped tightly at the forehead or chin gives less to work with.

Your photo checklist

Before you choose, run through these questions:

  • Are everyone's eyes open and easy to see?
  • Is the photo in focus when you zoom in on the faces?
  • Are there sunglasses, hats or hair covering part of a face?
  • Is anything blocking the people, like a hand, a drink or another guest?
  • Is the file the original, not one that has been shared through a chat app several times?
Zoom test Zoom in on the faces on your phone. If they still look clear, the photo is a strong candidate.

Groups, pets and old prints

Group photos can work well, but the more people there are, the smaller each face becomes. For a larger group, a photo where everyone is close together and at a similar distance from the camera helps. If you love two separate photos, ask whether they can be combined.

Pets are a favourite for crystals. Pick a photo where the pet is looking toward the camera with their face in good light. Dark fur in a dim room is harder to read than the same pet near a window.

For old printed photos, scan them or take a straight-on picture in good light, with no glare on the glossy surface. Avoid photographing a print at an angle, since that bends the faces.

Skip the heavy filters

Filters, beauty effects and strong colour edits can change the shape of a face or soften the details that matter. If you have both an edited version and the original, send the original. A natural photo almost always turns out better than a stylized one.

Send a few options

If you are torn between photos, send two or three. It is easier to compare them side by side than to pick one alone and hope. Tell us who is in the photo and what the crystal is for, whether that is a wedding, a new baby, a pet or a memorial. That context helps us suggest the best shape and layout for your gift.
