Favours are one of the easiest things to get wrong: too many and you are paying for leftovers, too few and someone goes without. Here is a simple way to plan it.

## 1. Count households, not guests

Couples and families usually share one favour. Take your guest list and count each couple or family as a single unit. That number is your starting point.

## 2. Add a small buffer

Add about 5 to 10 percent for late additions, the wedding party and a few spares. It is cheaper than a rush reorder.

> **Quick formula** Households + wedding party + 5 to 10% buffer = how many favours to order.

## 3. Pick something they will keep

The favours that get used are the ones with a purpose: a small engraved wood keepsake, a personalized glass or tumbler, a coaster, or a keychain with your names and date. Edible favours are lovely, but they disappear by the end of the night.

- Engraved coasters or small wood pieces
- Personalized glasses or tumblers
- Keychains with names and the date
- Matching gifts for the bridal party, such as robes, totes or tumblers

## 4. Order earlier than you think

Custom pieces are made to order, and designs need to be confirmed before anything is engraved or printed. Start the conversation a couple of months ahead when you can, and tell us the date up front so we can plan around it.

## 5. Keep the design simple

Names and a date, a short monogram or a small crest read well on almost any material. If you are matching other pieces at the event, use the same design across favours, signage and bridal party gifts so everything ties together.
