Teachers give a lot, and a thoughtful thank-you means more than a big one. The gifts they keep are the ones that feel personal and that they can actually use. Here is how to choose well.

## Think useful and personal

Teachers often receive the same few items every year. A gift with their name, a short message or a small detail that fits them stands out. Even a simple item feels special when it is made for one person.

## Gift ideas that work

- A personalized tumbler or water bottle for the school day
- A notebook or planner with their name or initials
- A small gift basket with a few favourite treats
- An engraved wood piece or coaster with a short thank-you
- A tote bag printed with their name or a short message

## Keep the message short

A name and a few words, such as thank you or a school year, read clearly and look neat. If you have more to say, write it on a card and tuck it in with the gift.

> **A good test** If the teacher would use it every week, it is a good choice.

## Consider a group gift

If the class wants to give one gift, one parent can collect the details and place a single order. A shared gift is often more memorable, and it saves the teacher from receiving a pile of small items. Share the class name and the number of people when you get in touch.

## Pick the right moment

Teacher gifts are common at the end of the school year and around the holidays, and some families give a small gift for a birthday or a special thank-you. Custom pieces are made to order, so the earlier you ask, the more options you will have. Tell us the date you need it by, and we will plan around it.

## What to send us

To get a quick quote, it helps to share:

- The teacher's name and how you would like it written
- The occasion and the date you need it by
- Whether it is one gift or a group gift
- Any favourite colours or interests
